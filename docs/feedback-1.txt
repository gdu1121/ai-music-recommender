Project specific comments are below:

    Research question of "Does combining collaborative filtering, audio-based features, and sequential listening behavior improve song recommendations compared with using each recommendation method individually?" is interesting and experimental setup is described.
    Topics: Collaborative filtering, CNN, RNN/LSTM, RL is proposed and is a potential path but scope might be too big.
        proposed combining multiple datasets with different identifiers, features, and audio availability may make the scope too big. Suggest the team select one primary dataset and establish popularity-based and collaborative-filtering baselines before introducing more complex models.
        Consider the comments of industry visitors.
        I suggest a reproducible hybrid recommender  that uses CNN or RNN/LSTM with a clearly defined feedback-learning experiment may be more viable.
        Also you might want to look at RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback), a core component of LLM post-training if explicit user preferences can be collected as pairwise song rankings and used to train a reward or preference model that improves recommendations.
    Will connect you all to Prof. Thais on evaluation and benchmark questions.
    Evaluation: suggest  controlled ablation experiments should measure the incremental benefit of audio features, listening sequences, and feedback-driven adaptation.
